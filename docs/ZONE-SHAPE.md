# The shape of a zone

Philip's standing spec (2026-08-31): **never make a square zone.**

> "Did you actually look at the zone map? The zone map of moonmere shores is not
> a square. NEVER make a square zone. Old zones were square, we don't care about
> those, we are in the beginning of a rebuild."

Every zone card in `design/the-empty-throne.html` (Zone Maps tab, generated by
`tools/design/gen_zonemaps.py`) draws an irregular plate: coasts on some edges,
survey lines and ridges on others. **That plate is the zone.** The 700m square
the terrain mesh is laid on is an implementation detail the player must never
be able to see.

## The spec

- **A new zone has a `ZoneShape`**: the card's polygon in world metres, plus
  one flag per EDGE saying whether that edge is water. The zone's module hands
  it to `registerZone({ shape })`, which files it in `ZONE_SHAPES`
  (`client/src/scene.ts`); `zones/caerdunfields.ts` is a typical one. Moonmere
  Shores' `MS_SHAPE` and Heartwood's are the two still declared in scene.ts.
- **A coast edge is where the sea starts.** `makeOcean` in `shore` mode measures
  from the shape instead of from an arc, so a plate can have water on three
  sides and land on the fourth — which an arc cannot say.
- **A land edge is where the rim chain runs.** `buildRimMountains` walks the
  shape's land edges; the coast edges get no chain at all (RIM.md: a chain
  standing in the sea is worse than a fogged horizon).
- **Nothing else marks the boundary.** No square bowl in the height function
  (`fieldHeight` skips it for a shaped zone — measured off the square, it raised
  a ridge across the middle of the water on one side and nothing on the other),
  no square fog trick, no invisible wall.
- **The map draws the same polygon** (`drawZoneBase` in `client/src/map.ts`).
  The map and the ground have to agree about where the zone stops.
- **Land is floored above the waterline.** The square zones got that from their
  edge bowl. Without it a plate's own field noise dips under the sea's level
  hundreds of metres inland, and every hollow is a place the depth rule refuses
  to let anyone walk — invisible, because the water sheet is masked by the
  coast. Only the beach ramp may cross the waterline.

## Converting a card

The cards are drawn in a 380x300 SVG viewBox. Read the zone's `poly` out of
`gen_zonemaps.py` — it is a list of `((x, y), style)`, where the style belongs
to the segment STARTING at that point — and map it with ONE uniform scale so
the plate is not distorted:

```
world_x = (svg_x - 197) * k        world_z = (145 - svg_y) * k
```

`k` is whatever fits the polygon inside the zone (2.36 for a 700m Moonmere
Shores). `coast` is `true` for the `"coast"` style and `false` for `"wild"`,
`"ridge"` and `"survey"`.

`shapeDetail()` then subdivides and wanders every edge on seeded noise, once, so
no boundary is a ruled line — and the coast, the rim and the map all read that
same wandered polygon.

## Measured constraints

- **A coast edge and a land border are a reading of the CARD, and the card can
  be read wrong.** Moonmere Shores' east edge was built as coast and is a land
  border (Philip): the shore road leaves through the corner where it meets the
  northern one, and a road cannot leave over water. When an exit sits on an
  edge, that edge is land.
- **`shoreRing` marches OUTWARD from its scan centre, and a plate's centre is
  dry land.** The march used to look only for a below→above crossing, which is
  what a centre out at sea finds — it starts in the water and comes up onto the
  beach. On a plate it crosses the other way, so the ring came back with twenty
  usable bearings out of three hundred and sixty and the beach had no foam on
  it at all. It now takes either transition.
- **Mirror the DIRECTION, not the numbers.** The wash's cross-section is
  written as offsets from the waterline, negative into the water, and the code
  that decides which stops follow the seabed and which are pinned to the
  waterline is keyed to that sign. Mirroring the stop list for a plate inverted
  that test and left the foam pinned flat where the water drew over it;
  `shoreBand`'s `mirror` flips the radial direction instead and the stops keep
  meaning what they say.
- **A coast RUNS ON past the corner where a land border takes over.** Otherwise
  the water stops dead on the line where the rim chain begins and the two edges
  line up into a visible seam (Philip: "where the ocean ends line up with the
  mountain rims, I want the ocean to be extended past and slightly around the
  rim mountains"). `shapeNear` extends each coast segment by `COAST_RUN_ON`
  (70m) at any end that meets a land edge, so the sea reaches behind the end of
  the chain and it reads as a headland. The chain's own `clearWhere` has to
  allow its ends to stand in the shallows, or it pulls back off the corner and
  reopens the seam it was hiding.
- **The coastline wobbles, so nothing may assume where it is.** `makeOcean`
  adds its own two-octave noise on top of the shape, which moves the waterline
  by ±20m. Every built thing on a shore — the causeway's landward end, the
  ferry berths, the harvest's beds, the shore buildings — takes its position
  from a MEASURED crossing (`waterlineZ` in the Moonmere Shores builder), not
  from the polygon. A guessed z put the causeway gate in the shallows.
- **`seaward()` is the distance to the WATER, not to the boundary.** They are
  different questions on a plate: a point twenty metres inside the northern land
  border is not twenty metres from the sea. Conflating them laid a beach ramp
  along that border and let the sand ring accept crossings up against the wall.
- **A beach on a plate is defined by `seaward`, not by a polar ring.**
  `vigSandBed`'s ring mask assumes the scan centre is OUT TO SEA, so "inside the
  ring" means water — true for a bay, upside down for a plate, whose centre is
  dry land and whose whole interior the mask would call beach. `makeBeach`
  passes its own coverage (and mirrors the foam's cross-section) when its ocean
  is shaped.

- **A plate may be redrawn inside its square, and then the card is stale.**
  The King's Gardens (2026-10-10, the wilds revamp) pulled its two north
  corners about 80 m north, `W(72, 78)` / `W(312, 70)` to `W(72, 38)` /
  `W(312, 36)` in `zones/kingsgardens.ts`, for about 1.2x the area its
  terraces needed. `size` stayed 700: the server clamps to the square, not the
  plate, so a reshape inside the square needs no data change, and the map
  draws the module's `shape`, not the card. The card in
  `design/the-empty-throne.html` still shows the old outline. No other wild
  zone changed its plate or its size.
- **An edge exit is a pass on a LAND edge** (RIM.md "Passes"): its portal
  stands on the shaped edge, at least 12 m inside the square, where the chain
  runs. A coast edge has no chain to cut.

**Moonmere Shores is the reference consumer** (`MS_SHAPE`): a plate with two
land borders — north and east, both carrying rim chain, with the Border Gate
(`ms_gate`) on one and the shore road's pass at the corner where they meet — and
water on the south and west.
